Pro Engineer — Assetto Corsa telemetry and a virtual race engineer

It reads your telemetry and tells you what to change.

Most telemetry tools hand you graphs and leave the interpreting to you. This one does the interpreting: it reads Assetto Corsa's shared memory sixty times a second, works out what your tyres, brakes, fuel and driving are actually doing, and says it in sentences — "fronts over 28.4 psi, take 0.3 out" rather than a line on a chart.

It runs in a fast terminal on your second screen and inside the game as a Custom Shaders Patch panel. Free, open source, no account, and nothing about you leaves your machine.

Over a stint it separates a car that understeers from a driver who turns in early, and refuses to answer before it has four laps. Smoothness, trail braking, coasting, grip usage, lockups and over-rotation, all counted rather than felt.

Consumption measured from your own laps, laps remaining, what you need to finish, how short you are, and the pace drop-off across the stint.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SAYS

Not a mock-up — these are the forms the engineer produces. The marker in front of each line is how sure it is, and the third one is it declining to guess.

engineer — live
$ ac_pro_engineer ● Fronts over 28.4 psi (target 27.5) >> Take 0.3 psi out of both fronts ◐ Rear inner edges 13 °C hotter than the outers >> Less negative camber at the rear confirm: the I/M/O spread next run out ○ understeer — the car, not the driving 9 a lap on every one of 5 laps, while the times moved about ● T3 cost 0.34 s — 14 m late on the brakes, −4 km/h at the apex

Free and open source. It reads Assetto Corsa's shared memory directly, analyses tyre temperatures and pressures, brake heat, fuel and how you are driving, and says something useful about it — in a fast terminal on your second screen and in the game itself through Custom Shaders Patch.

ON THE SECOND SCREEN

Nine tabs, every keyboard shortcut rebindable, and every on-screen hint printed from the binding — so it cannot tell you the wrong key.

WHERE THE TIME WENT

Corners are found in the trace — a stretch where lateral load stays up long enough to be a corner rather than a kink — so it needs no track data and works on mods.

It will not invent a comparison. A corner the reference lap does not have is drawn as no comparison rather than as a delta of zero, and braking in metres needs the track's length rather than a guess at it.

WATCHING SOMEONE ELSE DRIVE

A driver is on track and cannot look away. A friend on the same network runs the same program, sets it to receive, and sees their telemetry and their engineer's advice. 

# the driver
"overlay": { "broadcast_to": "192.168.1.42:9001" }

# the friend watching
"overlay": { "receive_from": "0.0.0.0:9001" }

One network, and that is all. Two houses behind two routers is NAT, which needs a forwarded port or a relay in the middle — not built. No discovery, no championship mode: you type an address. Off unless you set one, because this is telemetry about a person.

The same feed is plain JSON over UDP, ten times a second, so writing your own front end is about fifteen lines of Python.

EVERYTHING IT DOES

The whole list, folded by area. It is longer than most people expect from something that costs nothing — open whichever part matters to you.

Tyres and brakes 11
Tyre pressure per cornerLive, in psi, bar or kPa.
Inner / middle / outer temperatureAll three per corner, not just the middle. The spread says whether a tyre is leaning the right way, which is what camber advice is made of.
Distance from your target hot pressurePer corner, with front and rear targets set separately.
Cold pressure calculatorWhat to set in the garage to arrive at your hot target, from the ambient and track temperature.
Brake temperaturePer corner, with a configurable ceiling and an overheating verdict on the lap summary.
Tyre wearPer corner, as a percentage of the tread.
Remaining tyre life in lapsFrom the worst corner rather than the average — a set is finished when one corner is.
Camber advice per axleFrom the inner-minus-outer spread, keeping the sign. An outer edge running hot wants more negative camber; reading only the magnitude gives exactly the opposite advice.
Measured only under loadA tyre says nothing about camber on a straight — both edges run level there. The sample is taken while the car is actually cornering, and the verdict waits for a second of it.
Bottoming detectionCounted per corner from the suspension travel, with a ride-height verdict after the lap.
Stint lengthHow long you have been on this set, which is not the lap count.
The race engineer, while you drive 9
Plain-language advice"Fronts over 28.4 psi (target 27.5)" with what to do about it.
Grouped findingsFour cold tyres is one line. Fronts, rears, left side, right side, all four.
Severity rankingInfo, warning, critical, worst first — so the line you read at 200 km/h is the one that matters.
Up to eight linesWith a slider for how many reach the overlay, and the number actually sent printed under it.
Confidence on every lineA count of corroborating observations and their spread, not a number picked while writing the rule. One observation is never confident however extreme.
New advice looks differentFrom advice that has been on screen for four laps.
Cause and checkWhy it is happening, and what to look at next run to know whether the change worked.
Shift pointRev bar with a configurable offset from the limiter, so the light comes when you want it.
Fuel warningRaised when what is left falls under the laps you configured.
The lap debrief 7
What the engineer made of the lapPressures and temperatures against your own windows, camber per axle, brakes, and how it was driven — up to eight lines.
Three laps of historyPage back through them with the game paused, in the terminal and in the car.
Sector times against the session bestFour tenths spread across a lap is a shrug; four tenths in one sector is a corner to go and look at.
Compare with the previous lap or your bestBoth, because they answer different questions.
What is leftLaps of tyre life and laps of fuel, coloured by whichever runs out first.
A clean lap says soRather than leaving an empty column that reads as "the analysis did not run".
A lap that measured nothing says nothingZero pressures are not flat tyres. Every average that was never taken is treated as absent rather than as zero.
Corner-by-corner lap analysis 10
Corners found in the traceA stretch where lateral load stays up long enough to be a corner rather than a kink. No track data, so it works on mods and on tracks nobody has written a table for.
Where the time went, corner by cornerEach corner is charged the track from its own entry to the next corner's entry — where a bad exit is actually paid for — so the sections add up to the lap's own delta.
Only the losses that matterOne key hides every corner that cost less than a tenth.
Braking point in metresHow much later or earlier than the reference, measured on the straight before the corner.
Entry, minimum and exit speedAll three against the reference, per corner.
Throttle timingHow long after the apex you got back on the power, against the reference.
Matched by distance, never by indexTwo laps can detect a different number of corners. Comparing T5 to T5 by position in the list compares two different corners and invents a delta.
Chicanes stay two cornersA left never matches a right, however close together they are.
No comparison is not zeroA corner the reference lap does not have is drawn as no answer, because inventing one is worse than admitting it.
Direction, apex and peak loadPer corner, with the apex taken at the slowest point rather than the tightest — the timing is what this is for.
Laps, ghosts and traces 12
Ghost delta by distanceAgainst your own recorded best lap, not whatever reference the game picked.
Speed and RPM against timeThe whole lap, plotted.
Both pedals and steeringThrottle, brake and steering angle traces, so a lock-up or a correction is visible rather than remembered.
Track map from your own traceDrawn from the positions you actually drove, so it needs no track file.
Friction circleThe GG diagram: how much of the tyre's grip you are using, and in which direction you are wasting it.
Slip ratioAgainst throttle, so wheelspin is separable from a car that simply has no grip.
Damper histogramsSuspension travel distribution per corner.
Sector splitsOn tracks with two, three or four sectors, detected rather than assumed.
Theoretical optimal lapFrom your best sectors, which need not have come from the same lap.
Save and load lapsAs JSON with full metadata; load one back as a ghost on another day.
MoTeC-compatible CSV exportNamed after the car, track and lap.
Reference times per car and trackKept between sessions, and worked out from the car's own specifications when there is nothing to compare with yet.
How you are driving 12
The car or the drivingUndersteer in the same quantity on every lap while the lap times move about is not following the driving. One that comes and goes is.
It refuses to answer earlyBelow four laps it says so rather than guessing. A car that understeers and a driver who turns in too early produce the same trace.
SmoothnessHow steadily you use the controls, rather than how fast.
AggressionHow hard the car is being asked to work.
Trail brakingHow much brake is still on while the car is turning.
CoastingThe share of the lap on neither pedal — usually the cheapest time on the table.
Pedal overlapThrottle and brake at once, counted.
Full throttle percentageHow much of the lap is spent flat.
Grip usageHow close to the friction circle's edge you actually run.
Lockups and wheelspinCounted per axle, not guessed from the lap time.
Understeer and oversteer momentsCounted over the lap, so one moment is not a verdict.
Over-rotationSteering past what the corner needs, with the worst excess angle — the tyres are scrubbing and the lap time shows it.

Where this one wins, and where it does not. It is the only one here that is free, open, accountless and native on Linux, and the only one that rates its own confidence or declines to answer. It is also the only one that supports exactly one simulator — the others cover four to seven — and it has no AI coach, no pro reference laps and no community of a quarter of a million drivers behind it. If what you want is somebody else's fast lap to chase, one of the subscriptions is a better buy than this is.

What it deliberately does not do 4
Cross the internetSharing works on one network. Two houses behind two routers needs a relay, which is not built.
Read a setup you did not saveThe simulator does not publish the loaded setup, so a change cannot be attributed reliably. Better to say nothing than to guess.
Support another simulator yetThe architecture is a folder per game and the boundary is real. Nobody has written the second folder.
Overlay outside Custom Shaders PatchThere was a second, desktop-drawn overlay. It did not survive exclusive fullscreen, never appeared in VR, and was invisible to the game's own screenshots. It was removed rather than half-fixed.

INSTALL

Windows

Download, unzip, run. It finds Assetto Corsa, writes the in-game panel, and creates the shared memory the panel reads. Nothing to start in a particular order. 

# unzip anywhere, then
ac_pro_engineer.exe

Linux & Steam Deck

The desktop side works on its own. The overlay needs a small bridge running inside the game's Proton prefix, because only a Windows process in there can give the shared memory the name the game is allowed to open. 

./ac_pro_engineer

# for the in-game overlay, leave this running too
protontricks-launch --appid 244210 shm-bridge.exe

From source

git clone https://github.com/Rgosh/ac-pro-engineer.git
cd ac-pro-engineer
cargo run --release

Supported simulators

Assetto CorsaFull support — terminal and in-game overlay via Custom Shaders Patch.
Anything elseA folder under core/src/games/. The architecture is built for it and the boundary is real, but nobody has written the second folder — so: not yet, rather than "coming soon".

QUESTIONS

Is it free? Yes, MIT

Free and open source. No account, no subscription, and no telemetry collected about you.

Does it work on Linux or a Steam Deck?

Yes. The desktop side is native. The in-game overlay additionally needs a small bridge running inside the game's Proton prefix, because only a Windows process in there can give the shared memory the name the game is allowed to open. The application ships the bridge and can fetch a matching one for you.

Will it cost me frames?

The desktop side is a terminal application at about 0.1 % of one core with no GPU cost. The in-game panel draws and nothing else — every calculation happens outside the game, because Lua runs on the render thread where a millisecond is a sixth of the frame budget at 165 Hz.

Which simulators are supported?

Assetto Corsa today. The core reads a game through a boundary designed for more than one — a simulator is a folder rather than conditionals through the middle of the engineer — but no second folder is written, and we would rather say that than imply otherwise.

Do I need Custom Shaders Patch?

Only for the in-game overlay. Everything else — the dashboard, the analysis, the engineer, the strategy work — runs without it.

Can I use my own reference lap?

Yes. Laps are saved as JSON with full metadata and can be loaded back as a ghost on another day, and the corner analysis compares against whichever lap you choose.

Can a friend see my telemetry?

On the same network, yes — they set an address to listen on and see your numbers and your engineer's advice in their own panel. It is off unless you turn it on, because it is telemetry about a person.

Does it modify my game?

It writes one folder — its own overlay app — into the game's Lua app directory, and nothing else. It reads the game's shared memory, which is the interface the game publishes for exactly this.
