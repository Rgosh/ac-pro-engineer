🌐 v0.4.5 — Watch Somebody Else's Session

v0.4.5 lets one person drive and another person watch — not a summary of it, the
whole session, on every screen. Plus everything reported since the last release:
the tyre pressure the advice judged you against, the wing it recommended to cars
that have not got one, and the camber reason that argued the opposite of its own
advice. Website: https://proengineer.app


🌐 Somebody Else's Session, On Your Screens

Two people, two machines, one network. One drives; the other presses one key and
their own screens fill with the driving happening on the other machine — the
dashboard, the traces, the map, the corner table and the engineer.

* Every screen, not a copy of four sentences. The whole reading travels thirty
  times a second, so the watching copy finds the laps, builds the traces, draws
  the map's line and runs its own engineer over your driving. A spectator can
  ask the same questions the driver can.
* In their units and their language. The advice is written on the machine
  reading it, not on yours — your friend sees °F and English if that is what
  they use.
* Two keys. Open LAN, the new tenth tab, on 0. S shares, W watches. The port,
  the rate and being findable are all chosen for you.
* Nobody reads an IP address out loud. The copies announce themselves and appear
  in each other's list, with the car and the track beside the name. Put the
  cursor on somebody and press Enter.
* It works with the graphical version too, in either direction — terminal
  watching the window, window watching the terminal. One protocol as of this
  release.
* Nothing leaves your network. There is no server in the middle, because there
  is no server. What gets announced is a name, a role and a port — nothing about
  your driving, and being findable is a switch you can turn off.

Watching a friend in another city? It needs no relay and no port forwarding: a
mesh VPN (Tailscale or ZeroTier, both free for a handful of machines) puts both
computers on one private network wherever they are. The watcher presses W, the
driver types the watcher's mesh address, and that is it. About half a megabit a
second, one way — less than a video call.

For a coach watching a driver, a friend on the sofa, or a spare laptop beside
the rig running your session on its own screen.


⚙️ New: Settings → SHARING

The half you set once and never look at again: readings a second, only send
while I'm on track, how long a quiet link waits before saying so, whether you
are findable, and whether a session is accepted from the whole network or only
from this machine.

The LAN screen itself reports what is actually happening — who is sending, at
what rate, how old the picture is, and how many readings never arrived. If the
link is bad, you can see that it is the link.


🔧 Fixed

* The engineer now knows what car it is talking about. From a review here: "this
  app will always give you false information ... esp when it comes to tyre
  pressure". It was right, and here is why. Every car was judged against one
  target — 27.5 psi, which is a GT3's number — and against one alert band. A
  single-seater works at 21 psi. So a Formula driver sitting exactly on the
  pressure the car is meant to run at was told to inflate all four, on every
  lap. The class table has known each class's hot pressure since v0.4.0; the
  advice was the half that was not reading it. It is now, the alert band moves
  with it, and a number you have typed yourself still wins over the table.
* And it no longer tells a road car to add downforce. From the same review: "I'm
  setting up a road car, and its telling me to adjust front downforce." High
  speed understeer answered "More Front Wing" to everything with four wheels.
  Road and vintage cars get the mechanical half of the same fix now — softer
  front springs or anti-roll bar, softer rear for oversteer — and nothing
  invented to fill the gap. Advice about a part your car does not have is worse
  than no advice.
* The camber advice no longer argues with itself. Told to add negative camber on
  a car already at -3.5°, you would read the reason first — and it said "the
  outer shoulder is not being loaded through corners", which is the argument for
  doing the opposite. Too little negative camber means the tyre stands up and
  the outer shoulder takes the load, and that is what it says now. The message
  also names the edge — "outer edge hotter" — instead of "contact patch
  inefficient" beside a number you had to work out for yourself. Reported on the
  graphical version's thread; the engineer is shared, so both got the fix.
* "not a saved lap: missing field rpms". Choosing your own reference lap could
  refuse to open it. A field was added to the lap format and old files were
  never taught to survive it — so every lap saved before that release stopped
  loading, and the laps most worth keeping are the oldest ones. They open again,
  and the test that keeps it that way uses the exact file that failed.
* The demo now fills the Analysis tab. It only ever built one lap, and that tab
  is the half of the program that is about comparing — one lap means nothing to
  overlay, nothing to subtract and a flat delta. Five laps now, so everything on
  that screen does what it does in a real session.
* An empty mapping is no longer mistaken for a running game. On Linux the bridge
  creates the shared-memory pages when it starts and leaves them when the game
  quits, so the app could read zeros and believe a car was there.
* engineer_probe prints the tread it judged — inner, middle, outer, the
  inner-minus-outer every camber verdict is made of, and the camber the wheel is
  actually running. When advice is questioned there was no way to ask the
  program what it read, only what it concluded. Now there is.


📋 Also

* lan_probe — a diagnostic that says which of the four parts of the network is
  at fault: your machine's address, the port, the group the copies find each
  other on, and whether anything is arriving. Run it before anybody is waiting
  on you.
* Sharing and listening each have their own switch, so turning one off keeps the
  address instead of making you type it again.
* The Russian translation covers the new screens.
* Both machines need v0.4.5 to watch each other. There is no half-working mode
  with the previous release — the screen says so by name rather than calling the
  traffic noise.


💾 How to Update

Windows: launch your current version and let the Auto-Updater install v0.4.5.
Restart Assetto Corsa afterwards so it picks up the new panel.

Linux & Steam Deck: let the Auto-Updater install v0.4.5, then restart Assetto
Corsa. You do not need a new shm-bridge.exe this time — the telemetry frame did
not change in this release, so every bridge from v0.4.0 onward keeps working. If
you are coming from v0.3.x, press the button on the launcher's overlay card to
fetch one.

The launcher may note that your bridge was built for an older release. That is a
note, not a fault — it says so because it is worth knowing before something is
wrong. Fetch the matching one if you would rather have them in step.

For LAN: update both machines. Open LAN (key 0), one of you presses S and the
other W, and pick each other out of the list.

Prefer a clean install? The archive attached here contains both the Windows and
Linux builds, shm-bridge.exe, and a loose copy of the overlay. The same release
is on GitHub with a checksum beside every file:
https://github.com/Rgosh/ac-pro-engineer/releases/tag/v0.4.5

Not sure whether the panel is working? Press the diagnostics key on the launcher
for a full report: it names the application, the panel and the bridge, and tells
you which of the three does not fit.
